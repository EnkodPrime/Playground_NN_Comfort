Things to try

  • Train ~200 epochs, then start the loop. Watch the green band appear and the room settle into it. At night the band drops by several degrees on its own — nobody programmed a night setback; the network learned that sleeping people (duvet, 0.75 met) like it cooler.
  • Winter-only training. Set season coverage to winter, regenerate, retrain, then start the loop in July. The zone is wrong — the network never saw people dressed for summer. That is distribution shift, the standard failure of learned comfort models.
  • Remove inputs. Untick Time of day and retrain: sleeping comfort and evening comfort now fight each other, and the confusion matrix shows where the compromise lands. Untick everything except room temperature and you get a plain learned thermostat.
  • Draughts move the zone. Put Air movement on the Y axis: more moving air needs warmer air — the dotted (true) boundary tilts, and a trained network tilts with it. In the loop, open the window and watch T* jump up while the room cools.
  • Movement is metabolic rate. X axis T room, Y axis Move: active people want it cooler. During a party (🎉) the learned setpoint drops — more people, more activity, more internal heat all point the same way.
  • Change the taste, keep learning. Start the loop with keep learning from the votes on, then drag occupant preference towards warmer. New votes disagree with the old zone, and over the following simulated days the band slides up to follow the people.
  • Slow heaters need the walls. Switch to the masonry stove: most heat arrives as radiation into the walls, minutes late. The wall-temperature input is what lets the network judge comfort correctly while the air is still catching up.
  • Voters are noisy. Push vote inconsistency to 0.4: test accuracy falls, but the quick test against the noiseless model barely moves — the network averages the noise away, which is the whole point of learning from many votes.
